# Academic Vocabulary for the MU GRAD Exam

Reading is 40 of 120 points, and most missed reading questions trace back to
unknown words in the key sentence. Vocabulary is also the cheapest daily
habit: 15–20 minutes, every day.

## How to study (this matters more than the list)

1. **Use spaced repetition** — Anki (free) or Quizlet. 15 new words/day,
   review queue daily.
2. **Learn words in sentences**, never as isolated translations. Card front:
   the word in an English sentence with the word blanked. Back: the word +
   a short English definition + (optionally) Thai gloss.
3. **Learn word families together**: *analyze / analysis / analytical /
   analytically* — the Grammar section's word-form questions test exactly
   this.
4. Harvest words from your own reading practice: any word that blocked your
   understanding of a passage goes on a card that night.

## Core list: the Academic Word List (AWL)

The single best-fit list for this exam is the **Academic Word List**
(Coxhead, 570 word families) — it's drawn from exactly the kind of academic
prose the reading section uses. Free versions and ready-made Anki decks are
easy to find (search "Academic Word List Coxhead").

Work through it by sublist (Sublist 1 = most frequent):
Sublists 1–4 are the priority; 5–7 next; 8–10 only if time allows.

## Starter set: 120 highest-yield AWL words

Sublists 1–2, grouped by theme. Tick them off as they become automatic.

**Research & evidence:** analyze, approach, assess, assume, concept, data,
derive, establish, estimate, evidence, indicate, interpret, method,
research, significant, source, theory, valid, evaluate, consistent

**Argument & logic:** concept, conclude, consequent, contrast, contradict,
criteria, define, distinct, factor, hypothesis, imply, infer, issue,
justify, logic, perspective, principle, rational, relevant, thesis

**Change & process:** achieve, adapt, alter, decline, emerge, evolve,
expand, fluctuate, generate, impact, maintain, modify, occur, phase,
proceed, process, shift, transform, transition, trend

**Structure & systems:** category, component, element, feature, framework,
function, hierarchy, integrate, layer, mechanism, network, section, sector,
structure, system, dimension, domain, format, mode, sequence

**Quantity & degree:** approximate, constant, decrease, dominant, equivalent,
exceed, majority, margin, maximum, minimum, percent, proportion, range,
ratio, sufficient, sum, ultimate, virtually, substantial, marginal

**Academic life & society:** academy, administration, community, culture,
economy, environment, ethic, institute, labor, legal, policy, regulate,
resource, role, status, welfare, authority, commission, fund, income

## Connectors and stance words (reading AND writing points)

These carry the logic of a passage — reading questions about the writer's
view hinge on them, and using them correctly lifts your writing score:

- **Contrast:** however, whereas, nevertheless, conversely, despite,
  albeit, on the contrary
- **Cause/effect:** consequently, thereby, hence, thus, owing to,
  give rise to
- **Addition/support:** moreover, furthermore, likewise, in addition,
  similarly
- **Concession:** admittedly, granted, although, even though,
  notwithstanding
- **Stance/hedging:** arguably, presumably, allegedly, apparently,
  seemingly, so-called, purportedly (these signal the writer's attitude —
  a favorite question type)

## Grammar-section vocabulary traps

Small words the error-identification items love:

- *number of* (+ countable) vs. *amount of* (+ uncountable)
- *few/fewer* (+ countable) vs. *little/less* (+ uncountable)
- *rise* (no object) vs. *raise* (needs object)
- *affect* (verb) vs. *effect* (noun)
- *economic* (about the economy) vs. *economical* (cheap to run)
- *percent* (after a number) vs. *percentage* (no number)
