## Best-fit practice materials (by section)

The MU GRAD TEST's Listening/Grammar/Reading sections closely mirror
**TOEFL ITP** (Institutional Testing Program), so ITP materials are the most
format-accurate drills available:

- *Longman Preparation Course for the TOEFL ITP* — the standard prep book;
  its Structure & Written Expression chapters map almost 1:1 to the MU GRAD
  Grammar section.
- Official TOEFL ITP practice sets (ETS).

**Reading (academic register, ~500-word passages):**
- TOEFL iBT / IELTS Academic reading practice sets
- Free daily reading: The Conversation (theconversation.com),
  Scientific American, BBC Future — comparable register and length.

**Listening:**
- TED-Ed (short, academic, subtitled for checking)
- BBC 6 Minute English → step up to MIT OpenCourseWare lectures
- Podcasts at natural speed; listen twice to mirror the exam's replay rule.

**Writing:**
- IELTS Task 2 prompts are close in style; practice typed, 60-minute,
  ~300–350 words.
- Grammarly (free tier) *after* self-proofreading — compare what it catches
  vs. what you caught, and feed misses into your error log.

**Speaking (computer-based, no interlocutor):**
- TOEFL iBT independent speaking prompts — same "talk to a computer on a
  timer" condition.
- Record on your phone; the listen-back-and-fix-one-thing loop is the core
  drill.

## Thai-language prep options

Several Thai tutoring outfits run MU GRAD-specific courses (e.g., on
SkillLane and similar platforms). These can help for the Grammar section
shortcuts and past-exam familiarity, but self-study with format-accurate
materials plus timed simulations covers the same ground for free. If you do
take a course, pick one that includes **timed mock exams** — that's the part
that's hard to replicate alone.

## Free tools

- **Anki** — spaced-repetition flashcards for [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md)
- **Academic Word List** (Coxhead) — free online, with ready-made decks
- **YouGlish** (youglish.com) — hear any word pronounced in real sentences,
  useful for speaking practice
